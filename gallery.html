Standard MCMC methods

Markov chains that propose & accept
Random Walk Metropolis

Blind Gaussian hops, accepted by the density ratio — the simplest sampler there is.
AMH
Adaptive Metropolis[1]

Learns the proposal covariance from its own history as it runs.
Hamiltonian Monte Carlo[2]

Rolls a frictionless particle along gradients to take long, informed leaps.
No-U-Turn Sampler[2]

HMC that picks its own path length, stopping when the trajectory doubles back.
Metropolis-adjusted Langevin[3]

Nudges proposals uphill along the gradient, then corrects to stay exact.
ULA
Unadjusted Langevin[3]

Gradient plus noise, never rejecting — fast but slightly biased.
SGLD
Stochastic Gradient Langevin[9]

Langevin steps from noisy mini-batch gradients for large-scale learning.
SGLD-CV
SGLD with Control Variates[10]

Anchors the noisy gradient to the mode to cut its variance.
FUSE
Tuning-free ULA (FUSE)[11]

Sets its own step size by optimising over the space of measures.
H2MC
Hessian-Hamiltonian (H2MC)[4]

Uses curvature as well as gradient to shape anisotropic proposals.
GIBBS
Gibbs Sampling

Updates one coordinate at a time from its exact conditional.
DE-Z
Differential Evolution (Z)[7]

A population of chains proposes moves from each other’s differences.
MCHMC
Microcanonical HMC[8]

Constant-energy dynamics on a tuned manifold for efficient mixing.

Non-reversible samplers

Piecewise-deterministic, rejection-free
ZZ
Zig-Zag Sampler[12]

A particle moves in straight lines, flipping one velocity at a time.
BPS
Bouncy Particle Sampler[13]

Ballistic motion that reflects off the density’s gradient — no rejections.

Non-Markovian iterative methods

Particle ensembles & nested sampling
Stein Variational Gradient[5]

A cloud of particles moves as one, toward the density and apart from itself.
NS
Nested Sampling (RadFriends)[6]

Peels the distribution in shells of increasing likelihood.
